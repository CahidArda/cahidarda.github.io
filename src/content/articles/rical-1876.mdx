[Rical 1876](https://rical-1876.vercel.app) is a set of timelines where each person is a
vertical line and time flows down. When people take part in the same event, their lines
meet in a box, each sitting with the side they were on. A line ends where the person dies.
Hover a name or a box for details, or tap on a phone.

It started with the late Ottoman statesmen (the _ricâl-i devlet_), from the Young Ottomans
to the early Republic, and later grew more tabs. The code is on GitHub: [CahidArda/rical-1876](https://github.com/CahidArda/rical-1876).

## Where it came from

I was in Macedonia recently and spent the trip reading about early 20th century Ottoman
history. We hear these names all through history class: Midhat Paşa, Enver, Talât, Niyazi,
Mahmud Şevket Paşa. But they tend to feel like characters who appear out of nowhere, do one thing
and leave, because it's hard to connect the same names across several events. I wanted to
see them next to each other.

## What surprised me

How often it was the same people. Names I knew from separate chapters of the history
books (1908, the Balkan Wars, the First World War, the War of Independence) turned out to
be a fairly small group who kept crossing paths over a few decades, sometimes on the same
side and sometimes not.

I had learned all of these events separately. Seeing who was who, and what they did,
laid out in one picture made them much easier to follow than any list of dates.

Once the Ottoman one worked, I added more tabs with the same habit of allies turning into
enemies: the late Roman Republic, the United States from the Revolution to the Civil War, the
French Revolution and the Bolsheviks.

## The photo rule

I ran into an unexpected blocker while building the app. I wanted pictures of the people and
events in the tooltips, and my agent refused to add them because of a rule in its
instructions:

> Claude doesn't download a photo of a public figure that it finds on the web, or put it
> into a slide, document or other file it makes for the person, whether as a copy or as a
> link.

It suggested I find the image URLs myself, which isn't realistic for hundreds of images. I
ended up convincing it by pointing out that what I wanted weren't photos but paintings,
busts and engravings, which it accepted, thankfully. Photographs stayed out, so most of the
Ottoman cast, who lived in the age of the studio photograph, still has no picture.

## How it is drawn

It is a single HTML file with no build step. This kind of chart is a
[storyline visualization](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/design-considerations-optimizing-storyline-visualizations),
first drawn by hand in xkcd's [Movie Narrative Charts](https://xkcd.com/657/), and it turns
into spaghetti fast. The layout follows the two steps of
[StoryFlow](https://shixialiu.com/publications/storyflow/index.html) (Liu et al., 2013):

- **Order** the lines in each event to minimize crossings, with the barycenter heuristic
  from [layered graph drawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layered_graph_drawing).
- **Straighten** them: move each line sideways as little as possible while keeping gaps
  and staying out of boxes it isn't part of, solved per event with
  [isotonic regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotonic_regression). Lines that
  aren't in an event don't move at all.
