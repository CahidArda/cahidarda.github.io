I made a small app called [Rical 1876](https://rical-1876.vercel.app). It is a timeline
of the late Ottoman and early Republican statesmen (the _ricâl-i devlet_), from the
Young Ottomans to the first years of the Republic. Each person is a vertical line and
time flows down. When people take part in the same event, their lines meet in a box, each
sitting with the side they were on. A line ends where the person dies.

The code is on GitHub: [CahidArda/rical-1876](https://github.com/CahidArda/rical-1876).

## Where it came from

I was in Macedonia recently and spent the trip reading about the events of the early
20th century: Resen, Manastır, 1908. Somewhere along the way the Çerkes Hasan incident
came up, and I wondered who he had shot and why.

We hear these names all through history class: Midhat Paşa, Enver, Talât, Niyazi,
Mahmud Şevket Paşa. But since we didn't live back then, they feel like characters who
appear out of nowhere, do one thing and leave. Who were they really, once you put them in
context? Who stood next to whom, and who turned on whom later? This timeline is an attempt
at that: the statesmen of the period, placed in relation to each other.

## What surprised me

How often it was the same people. The man who goes to the mountains in 1908 marches on
Istanbul in 1909, storms the Sublime Porte in 1913 and runs the war in 1914. The officer
sent off to Sofia in 1913, a polite way of getting him out of the capital, is the one
landing at Samsun in 1919. The opposition of 1912 is the government that loses the
Balkan War, and the man behind the 1876 constitution is later tried for the death of the
sultan he helped depose.

I had learned all of these events separately. Seeing who was who, and what they did,
laid out in one picture made them much easier to follow than any list of dates.

## How it is drawn

It is a single HTML file with no build step. This kind of chart is a
[storyline visualization](https://ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/design-considerations-optimizing-storyline-visualizations),
first drawn by hand in xkcd's [Movie Narrative Charts](https://xkcd.com/657/), and it turns
into spaghetti fast. The layout follows the two steps of
[StoryFlow](https://shixialiu.com/publications/storyflow/index.html) (Liu et al., 2013):

- **Order** the lines in each event to minimize crossings, with the barycenter heuristic
  from [layered graph drawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layered_graph_drawing).
- **Straighten** them: move each line sideways as little as possible while keeping gaps
  and staying out of boxes it isn't part of, solved per event with
  [isotonic regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotonic_regression). Lines that
  aren't in an event don't move at all.
