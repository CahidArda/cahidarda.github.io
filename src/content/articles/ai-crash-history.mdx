On October 6, 2026, Nvidia closed at a record \$239.24. Seven weeks later it closed at \$150.49, 37%
lower, after a low of \$125.53 on November 20
([Yahoo Finance price history](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NVDA/history/)). In between, its
co-founder and CEO resigned and came back
([8-K, November 11](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvda-20261111.htm);
[8-K, November 23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000090/nov23pressrelease.htm)),
Anthropic's shares fell below their IPO price
([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/anthropic-stock-ipo-price.html)), and inflation came in
hot ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/pce-inflation-report-october-2026.html)).

The pattern has come up before: a new technology, a fast-rising stock, years of orders on the books,
then a crash. The four figures below set the current sell-off next to earlier ones.

## The same shape

Each line below is one boom, drawn as a share of its own peak and lined up at that peak.

</div>

British railway shares, in Gareth Campbell and John Turner's index of every listed railway, doubled
between January 1843 and August 9, 1845. Then they fell for almost five years, to a low on April 16,
1850 that was 67% below the peak
([Campbell & Turner, 2010](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/21820/1/MPRA_paper_21820.pdf)). Seven
weeks after the peak they were still within 3% of it: the index "stayed close to that level for two
months", and the slide began in late October 1845.

Cisco, the company that sold the routers of the internet boom, closed at \$80.06 (split-adjusted) on
March 27, 2000, and at \$8.60 on October 8, 2002: 89% lower. It closed above its 2000 high again
only on December 10, 2025, more than 25 years later
([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CSCO/history/)). Seven weeks after their peaks,
Nvidia is at 63%, Cisco was at 78% and the railways at 98%. Nvidia's line ends at its latest close,
on November 27, 2026.

## Years of orders on the books

In October 2025 Jensen Huang said Nvidia
had \$500 billion of bookings for its Blackwell and Rubin chips through 2026
([Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidia-build-ai-supercomputers-us-191932227.html)).
Aircraft makers also sell years ahead. Boeing has orders for over 6,300 commercial airplanes worth
\$612 billion ([Q3 2026 results](https://boeing.mediaroom.com/2026-10-27-Boeing-Reports-Third-Quarter-Results)),
about ten years of work at its 2025 pace of 600 deliveries a year. Airbus ended 2025 with 8,754
aircraft on order after delivering 793
([Airbus](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-01-airbus-reports-793-commercial-aircraft-deliveries-in-2025)).

Orders can be canceled before delivery. Boeing's order book shrank after the 737 MAX was grounded in
2019 and air travel collapsed in 2020.

<div class="fx-figure">
  <OrderBook client:visible />
</div>

In 2020 Boeing's net orders were minus 1,026 airplanes. Buyers canceled orders for some 641 737 MAX
jets, and Boeing took another 523 off its books because it no longer expected them to be delivered
([Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/boeing-limps-2021-more-737-160625240.html)).
Shipyards went through the same thing in 2008. Their order books held over 10,000 ships, and by the
middle of the year buyers had started to cancel
([UN News, on UNCTAD's 2008 review](https://news.un.org/en/story/2008/11/280482)).

## Money that goes round

In both the telecom boom and the AI boom, suppliers have helped pay for their customers' purchases.
In 1999 telephone equipment makers lent money to young carriers so that the carriers could buy their equipment.
By the end of that year Lucent had agreed to lend about \$8.4 billion to customers, "including PCS
and wireless operators"
([Lucent 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001006240/000095011600000226/0000950116-00-000226.txt)).

<div class="fx-figure">
  <Loop client:visible />
</div>

One of those borrowers, the wireless carrier Winstar, filed for bankruptcy in April 2001, and Lucent
"fully reserved for its loans to Winstar"
([Lucent 8-K, April 2001](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001006240/000095012301501063/y48236e8-k.txt)).
That same quarter Cisco wrote off \$2.25 billion of inventory it had bought for orders that never came
([Cisco 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000858877/000089161801500994/f73006e10-q.txt)).

The 2026 arrangements take other forms. In February, Reuters reported that Nvidia was close to a \$30 billion
investment in OpenAI, in place of the up to \$100 billion it had announced in September
([PYMNTS, citing Reuters](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/nvidia-nears-30-billion-stake-in-record-breaking-openai-funding-round/)).
Nvidia's own filing describes guarantees on OpenAI's data center leases "capped at \$105 billion in the
aggregate" ([Nvidia 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm)).
It has also committed to buy \$390 billion of supply, mostly memory, over the next three years
([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidias-revenue-hits-usd115-8-billion-as-vera-rubin-generates-usd20-billion-in-another-record-setting-quarter-commitments-increase-to-usd390-billion-fy28-forecast-raised)).
Money lent or invested this way is recovered only if the customer earns enough from its own users,
subscribers in 1999 and ChatGPT and API customers today, to cover the cost of the hardware.

## What the crash leaves behind

The figure compares how long the assets built in each boom stay in service or on the books.

<div class="fx-figure">
  <AssetLife client:visible />
</div>

The railway projects approved in 1844 to 1846 built about 6,220 miles of line. Britain's whole network
today is around 11,000 miles, and big companies bought up the failed lines cheaply after the crash
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Mania)). In 2001 only about 5% of the new fiber in
the ground was in use
([Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/2001/6/18/19591977/economy-s-slump-idles-95-of-fiber-optic-lines/)),
but a cable maker expects it to last 25 to 40 years
([Prysmian](https://ro.prysmian.com/sites/ro.prysmian.com/files/media/documents/Product%20lifetime.pdf)).
Airlines depreciate a jet over 25 to 30 years
([United 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/100517/000010051719000009/ual_201810k.htm)).
The big cloud companies depreciate their servers over five to six years
([Microsoft](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000156459022026876/msft-10k_20220630.htm),
[Alphabet](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204425000014/goog-20241231.htm),
[Meta](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm)),
and Amazon shortened that from six years to five in 2025, citing the pace of AI
([Amazon 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000004/amzn-20241231.htm)).
On those schedules, servers bought in 2025 are fully depreciated by around 2030.

## Where the booms differ

The railway companies of the 1840s and the carriers of 1999 were new firms running on borrowed money.
Most AI chips are bought by Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta, which make large profits. FactSet expects their 2026 free cash flow to be "close to zero or turn
negative for all except Alphabet and Microsoft", and more of the spending is paid for with debt
([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/hyperscalers-tap-external-financing-as-ai-capex-outruns-cash-flow)).
AI-related borrowers
have issued nearly a quarter of non-financial US dollar bonds this year
([PIMCO](https://www.pimco.com/us/en/insights/the-credit-market-lens-hyperscalers-are-repricing-not-displacing-so-far)).
Nvidia's revenue also kept growing: it was \$115.8 billion last quarter, double a year
earlier ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027)).
