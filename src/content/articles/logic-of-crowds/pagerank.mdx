A recommendation from someone you trust counts for more than ten from strangers. PageRank, the
idea behind Google's early ranking, applies the same rule to the web: a page is important if
important pages link to it.

## A surfer who never stops

That definition is circular, so picture a surfer instead. They start on some page and click a
random link, again and again. Every so often they get bored and jump to a page chosen at random.
Count where they spend their time.

<div class="fx-figure">
  <RandomSurfer client:visible />
</div>

The visit counts settle on fixed numbers, and those numbers are the PageRank. A has six
in-links, the most of any page. B has two, yet B ends up on top: A links only to B, so whenever
the surfer leaves A by a link, they land on B. (Drag the link chance $s$ below about 0.7 and the
random jumps dilute that one strong link enough for A to win back.)

## Rank flows like water

The same numbers come out of a simple update rule, the one chapter 14 of Easley and Kleinberg's
[_Networks, Crowds, and Markets_](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/networks-book/networks-book.pdf)
builds up. Give each of the $n$ pages $1/n$ of the rank. At every step, each page splits its rank
equally among the pages it links to.

<div class="fx-figure">
  <RankFlow client:visible />
</div>

The basic rule has a weak spot. A group of pages that link only to each other is a spider trap:
rank flows in and never comes back out, until the trap holds all of it. The fix is the surfer's
boredom. Each page passes on only a fraction $s$ of its rank, and the rest is shared equally by
every page:

<EqTip note="Page i gets an equal share (1-s)/n of the leftover rank, plus a fraction s of the rank of every page j that links to it, divided by the number of links on j.">

$$
r_i \;\leftarrow\; \frac{1-s}{n} \;+\; s \sum_{j \to i} \frac{r_j}{\text{out}(j)}
$$

</EqTip>

This is the scaled update rule from section 14.3 of the book, and it gives the same answer as the
surfer who follows a link with probability $s$ and jumps to a random page otherwise. Repeat the
update and the ranks converge, whatever they started at.

<Lesson>

You are important when important pages point to you.
