Two drivers meet on a narrow bridge. Each one's best move depends on what the other does. A game
is just that: players, their strategies, and a payoff for every combination of choices
(chapter 6 of Easley and Kleinberg's
[_Networks, Crowds, and Markets_](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/home/kleinber/networks-book/networks-book.pdf)).

## Follow the arrows

Each cell below is one outcome: blue's payoff, then red's. An arrow means one player can do better
by switching on their own. Blue moves up and down, red moves left and right. A cell with no arrow
leaving it is a **Nash equilibrium**: every player is already giving their best response to the
others.

<EqTip note="u_i is player i's payoff. s* is the equilibrium profile; s_-i means everyone except i. No single player can raise their own payoff by switching alone.">

$$
u_i(s_i^*, s_{-i}^*) \ge u_i(s_i, s_{-i}^*) \quad \text{for every player } i \text{ and every strategy } s_i
$$

</EqTip>

In the Prisoner's Dilemma, Defect is a **dominant strategy**: it is the better reply to anything
the other player does, so both blue arrows and both red arrows point at it. Most games have no
dominant strategy. The stag hunt and the coordination game have two equilibria, and the arrows
alone cannot say which one people will reach.

## When the arrows never stop

In Matching Pennies, the token never rests. The way out is to randomize. If red shows Heads with
probability $q$, blue's two moves pay the same exactly when

$$
q \cdot 1 + (1-q)(-1) = q(-1) + (1-q) \cdot 1 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad q = \tfrac12
$$

<div class="fx-figure">
  <MixedWay client:visible />
</div>

When both players flip a fair coin, neither can do better by changing their odds. That is a
**mixed equilibrium**, and John Nash [proved in 1950](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.36.1.48) that every finite game has at least one
equilibrium once mixing is allowed.

## Stable is not the same as good

An equilibrium only says nobody wants to move alone. It says nothing about whether the outcome is
good. An outcome is **Pareto-optimal** when you cannot make one player better off without making
the other worse off.

<div class="fx-figure">
  <ParetoPlane client:visible />
</div>

Mutual cooperation in the Prisoner's Dilemma is better for both, and it is not an equilibrium. The
[next page](/articles/logic-of-crowds/prisoners-dilemma) is about getting there anyway.

<Lesson>

An equilibrium is a place where everyone is already doing their best given what the others do. It
is stable, which does not make it good.

</Lesson>
