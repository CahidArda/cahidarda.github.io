Everyone who played the Minesweeper that came with Windows has stared at a corner of numbers and wondered
whether it even makes sense. In 2000 Richard Kaye, a mathematician at the University of Birmingham, proved
that this question is NP-complete: as hard as every problem in [NP](/articles/how-hard-is-hard/np)
([Kaye, _The Mathematical Intelligencer_ 22(2), 2000](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03025367)).

## Is this board possible?

Kaye's question is not "can you win" but consistency: given the numbers, "to determine if there is some pattern
of mines in the blank squares that give rise to the numbers seen." Here is his own 6 by 6 example.

</div>

A proposed placement is checked in one pass over the numbers. Finding one blind is another matter: each grey
square is a mine or not, so

$$
2^{20} = 1{,}048{,}576 \text{ placements} \qquad 2^{480} \approx 3 \times 10^{144} \text{ on an Expert board}
$$

The second tab is the lopsided part. A "yes" comes with proof you can check; a "no" does not. Kaye: "what might
we guess to show inconsistency?"

## Circuits made of mines

To prove it hard, Kaye built logic out of numbers. In his wire the numbers allow exactly two fillings, mines on
every $x$ or on every $x'$, so the wire carries TRUE or FALSE. A NOT gate shifts the pattern.

<div class="fx-figure">
  <Wire client:visible />
</div>

With wires, bends, splitters, NOT and an AND gate, any logic circuit becomes a board only polynomially larger.
A fast consistency checker would then decide whether any circuit can output TRUE (the SAT problem), and by the
[Cook-Levin theorem](/articles/how-hard-is-hard/np-complete) every problem in NP with it. That would settle
[P vs NP](/articles/how-hard-is-hard/p-vs-np). The same spring the Clay Institute put
[a million dollars](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/pvsnp.pdf) on that question, and Ian
Stewart's column became ["Million-Dollar Minesweeper"](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/million-dollar-minesweeper/).
Kaye's own ending was calmer: "there may still be an efficient algorithm for Minesweeper, and finding it could
solve one of mathematics's most important open problems."

<Lesson>

Checking a Minesweeper board is easy. Deciding whether any board is possible is as hard as everything in NP,
because mines can be wired into circuits.

</Lesson>
