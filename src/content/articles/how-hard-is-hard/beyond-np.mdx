Show someone a solved Sudoku and they can check it in a minute. Now try to convince them that
White wins a chess position. A single game proves nothing, because Black might have played
better. This is the gap between [NP](/articles/how-hard-is-hard/np) and the classes above it:
**PSPACE**, **EXPTIME**, and their neighbours.

## Why games are harder

A small game shows the difference. Four stones, each player takes one or two, and whoever takes
the last stone wins.

A puzzle asks whether there _exists_ a good sequence of moves. A game asks whether there is a
move for you such that _for every_ reply there is a move for you, and so on:

$$
\exists m_1 \; \forall m_2 \; \exists m_3 \; \forall m_4 \; \cdots \; \text{you win}
$$

As Scott Aaronson puts it, "there's no obvious NP witness for (say) White having a win in chess,
since one would need to specify White's response to every possible line of play by Black"
([_P =? NP_, p. 20](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/pnp.pdf)). If the game ends within a
polynomial number of moves, you can still search the whole tree with little memory, one branch at
a time. That puts it in PSPACE. Generalized geography, a word-chain game played on any graph, is
one of PSPACE's hardest problems (Sipser, section 8.3).

Without a move limit, chess on an $n \times n$ board can last exponentially long, and Fraenkel and
Lichtenstein proved that playing it perfectly takes time exponential in $n$
([_J. Combin. Theory A_, 1981](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165%2881%2990016-9)).

## The map

<div class="fx-figure">
  <HarderMap client:visible />
</div>

Every box sits inside the next one, and that much is proven:

$$
\mathsf{P} \subseteq \mathsf{NP} \subseteq \mathsf{PSPACE} \subseteq \mathsf{EXPTIME}, \qquad \mathsf{P} \neq \mathsf{EXPTIME}
$$

The second part is the time hierarchy theorem: more time lets you solve strictly more problems
(Hartmanis and Stearns, 1965; Sipser, section 9.1). So at least one of the three steps in the
chain is a real gap. Nobody knows which.

Two boxes cut across the chain. coNP is NP's mirror image: a "no" answer has a short proof.
BQP is what a quantum computer does fast. It contains
[factoring](/articles/how-hard-is-hard/factoring)
([Shor, 1994](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9508027)), and most researchers conjecture it does
not contain the NP-complete problems (Aaronson, conjecture 34). Outside all of them is the
[halting problem](/articles/how-hard-is-hard/halting), which no amount of time solves.

<Lesson>

Checking one answer is NP. Beating every reply is PSPACE. Some problems provably need exponential
time, and some have no algorithm at all.

</Lesson>
