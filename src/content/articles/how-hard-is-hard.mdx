Sorting a million names takes a laptop less than a second. Finding the shortest trip through fifty cities could
outlast the sun. Checking a finished Sudoku is easy; filling in a blank one is not. Complexity theory is the study
of that difference: which problems get only a little harder as they grow, and which ones explode.

Its most famous open question, **P versus NP**, asks whether every problem whose answer is easy to check is also
easy to solve. Nobody knows, and there is a million-dollar prize for the answer.

These pages build the ideas from pictures you can play with, in the spirit of the
[game theory series](/articles/logic-of-crowds). The concepts follow Michael Sipser's _Introduction to the Theory
of Computation_ (3rd ed., 2012), the standard first course on the subject.
