Two suspects, two choices, one of the most studied games in all of science. Don't read about
it — play it. Every idea below is a thing you do.

## The matrix

Both of you choose **Cooperate** or **Defect** at the same time. The dilemma is built into
four numbers — temptation $T$, reward $R$, punishment $P$, sucker $S$ — bound by two conditions:

$$T > R > P > S \qquad 2R > T + S$$

## One round

No second chance. Pick.

Whatever they do, defecting pays you more: $\text{Defect}$ weakly dominates. So the only Nash
equilibrium is mutual defection $(P,P)$ — and it leaves you both worse off than mutual
cooperation $(R,R)$. That gap is the dilemma.

## Survival of the fittest

Let the strategies breed in proportion to their score. The whole population follows the
replicator equation,

$$\dot{x}_i = x_i\big(f_i(\mathbf{x}) - \bar f(\mathbf{x})\big)$$

where $x_i$ is the frequency of strategy $i$, $f_i$ its average payoff, and $\bar f$ the
population mean: do better than average and you grow. Press play — and try the presets.

In a quiet world, win-stay/lose-shift takes over. Add enough mistakes and cooperation dies. Run
it long enough and you get both: dominance, then collapse, then dominance again — the same
restlessness I [watched at 100,000 generations](/articles/prisoners-dilemma-rl/) when I first
built this simulation in Java.
