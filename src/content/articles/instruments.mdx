I walked into an exhibition of old instruments in Istanbul expecting to look at some brass for
twenty minutes. I stayed for most of the afternoon. There was a wood-and-brass pendulum clock by
Mustafa Şem'i, a pair of quadrants for telling the time from the sun, a sextant next to an
engineer's treatise on how to use it, a theodolite still in its box with a Constantinople
maker's label, and a hand-drawn quadrant from 1848 with one red line across the grid.

The labels said what each object was. They did not say how it worked, or what people did before
it existed, or what replaced it. Those were the questions I kept asking, so this series is my
attempt to answer them, with drawings you can move.

There are three posts, one per thing being measured:

- **Time.** From sundials and water clocks to pendulum clocks, marine chronometers and atomic
  clocks.
- **Latitude.** How far north or south you are, from the noon shadow of a stick to the sextant.
- **Heights and distances.** How tall a minaret is and how far away the other shore is, from
  plumb bobs and triangles to GPS.

Each one starts with the crudest method that worked and climbs to the current state of the art.
The museum objects are rungs on the way up.
